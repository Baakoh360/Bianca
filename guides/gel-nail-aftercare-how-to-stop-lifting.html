Gel nail aftercare: how to stop lifting and chipping

Most gel nails lift because of water, oils or knocks, not because the product failed. A few small habits help a manicure last its full two to three weeks.

By The Beauty Journal editors · Updated 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

On this page

  1. Daily habits
  2. What shortens a manicure
  3. When to book your refill
  4. FAQ

Daily habits

  • Cuticle oil every day. It keeps the nail and skin flexible, so there's less cracking and lifting.
  • Hand cream after washing, avoiding the nail edge where gel meets the nail.
  • Gloves for washing up and cleaning products.
  • Use the pads of your fingers to open cans and packages, not your nails.

What shortens a manicure

  • Long soaks in baths, pools or hot tubs
  • Harsh sanitisers and acetone-based products on the gel
  • Peeling or picking at lifting edges, which tears layers off your nail
  • Very long nails, which snag and snap more easily
Tip: if a nail starts to lift, book a repair. Water and bacteria can get under the gap.

When to book your refill

Around two to three weeks, or sooner if you see lifting. Waiting longer puts pressure on the growth area. If you want to remove your gel at home, follow our safe removal guide.

Seek medical advice if the skin around a nail is red, swollen or painful.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my gel nails lift at the cuticle?

Common causes are oils or moisture on the nail during application, gel placed on the skin, or nails growing out. Tell your technician if it keeps happening.

Can I file my gel nails at home?

You can gently shape the free edge with a fine file. Don't file the surface or cuticle area.

How long should gel nails last?

Usually two to three weeks. After that, growth makes the edge visible and lifting more likely.

This guide is educational and not medical advice. Always patch test new products, follow your technician's instructions and seek professional help if you have pain, swelling or an allergic reaction.
