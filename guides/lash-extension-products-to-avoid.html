Lashes

Lash extension products to avoid (and what to use instead)

Lash extension adhesive doesn't like oil. A product that's fine on bare lashes can loosen a whole set within days, so it pays to check labels.

By The Beauty Journal editors · Updated 5 October 2026 · 3 min read

On this page

  1. What to avoid
  2. What to use instead
  3. Pairing with your routine
  4. FAQ

What to avoid

  • Oil-based makeup removers and cleansing balms near the eyes. Oils break down the adhesive.
  • Waterproof mascara, which needs oil to remove.
  • Heavy eye creams and eye makeup with oils applied close to the lash line.
  • Cotton wool and fluffy pads, which snag and leave fibres on the lashes.
  • Metal lash curlers, which can bend or break extensions.
  • Harsh peels or exfoliants that touch the lash line.
Tip: read the ingredient list. Oils such as mineral oil, coconut oil or "paraffinum liquidum" are a warning sign.

What to use instead

  • A foaming lash cleanser designed for extensions
  • A clean spoolie to brush through when dry
  • Oil-free skincare and makeup remover around the eyes
  • Lint-free pads or a soft brush for cleansing

Pairing with your routine

Cleanse gently every few days, pat dry and brush. See the full steps in our lash and nail care guide.

If you notice itching, redness or swelling around your eyes, stop using the product and seek advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use any mascara with lash extensions?

Most technicians advise against it. If you do, choose a water-based, non-waterproof formula and apply only to the tips. Ask your technician first.

Is micellar water safe?

Oil-free micellar water used gently on the skin around the eye can be fine, but don't soak the lashes. Check the label for oils.

Can I use eyelash growth serums?

Check with your technician. Some oily serums may weaken the bond.

This guide is educational and not medical advice. Always patch test new products, follow your technician's instructions and seek professional help if you have pain, swelling or an allergic reaction.
